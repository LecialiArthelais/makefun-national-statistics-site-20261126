Reading the environment

The forest is the asset. At 50.7% of land area, Indonesia is one of the world’s most forested large countries — and the share has barely moved in a decade. Keeping it flat is itself the achievement, given the pressure from palm oil, mining and settlement.

Emissions are low per person, high in total. 2.87 tonnes per capita is modest beside the G20 average, but 283.5 million people add up to 812.2 million tonnes. The energy mix explains why: coal at 40.37% is the single largest source, and it is also the country’s biggest export.

The transition has started, slowly. New and renewable energy reached 14.65% of primary energy in 2024. The direction is set; the pace is the open question — and the one this page will track year by year.
